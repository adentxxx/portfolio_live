Spendix – Personal Finance

A mobile app for tracking personal finances with shared family access.

#Fintech#Mobile

Task

Design a mobile app that makes it simpler to keep a personal and a family budget. Regular tracking shouldn’t feel like a chore. Analytics and small interface reactions bring the person back to the app. It works for one person as well as for a family or a couple.

Survey and app review

Survey

Thirty people aged 20–35 took a survey in Google Forms. The answers showed three barriers: • Complex, overloaded interfaces. • Most services have no family mode: shared spending and each person’s contribution aren’t visible. • Few reasons to log expenses regularly: it’s a routine with no feedback. The app’s scenarios and structure grew out of this.

Competitor analysis

I looked at ZenMoney, Spendee, Splitwise, CoinKeeper, and Money Manager. I compared which features are up front, what happens on first launch, whether there is shared tracking, and what brings a person back to the app. What they get wrong: • Almost none has a full “family” mode. • Onboarding is either overloaded or doesn’t explain how the app works. • Tracking and statistics are there, but the habit of managing money regularly is not. • Weak visual hierarchy, dated interfaces, no adaptation to different screens. From this, Spendix took simplicity, a shared budget, and gentle reasons to come back.

Design decisions

Family mode

You can add family members, see shared and personal spending, compare statistics, and share responsibility for the budget.

Motivation for regular tracking

Motivation is there to bring the person back more often, without pressure. Later, this behavior can be tuned to how regularly they enter data. Examples: • Dashboards that react: “You’re within the plan!” or “Café spending is up 40%”. • Badges for regular tracking: 3 days in a row, a week without overspending. • Reminders that fit the person’s habits, with no officialese. • “Free money” at the end of the month, based on a forecast.

Mobile-first logic

Adding an expense or opening the balance takes one or two taps.

Financial analytics

Dashboards: spending by category, forecasts, balances, debts.

Onboarding and empty states

I thought through the first visit to the app. Every area has its own empty screen: it says why it’s empty and suggests the next step. • On first launch, the app offers family mode. • On the home screen, the empty state asks for the first entry. • In analytics, the empty state explains that numbers will appear later.

Also in the product

Focus on current data

The user sees cards with upcoming expenses, debts, financial goals, and the remaining budget, with no extra navigation or overload.

Tagging entries

A transaction can be marked with a tag, for example “shared”, “kids”, or “health”. That makes reports easier to put together.

Smart notifications

Reminders adapt to how the person uses the app. For example: “It’s already mid-month, and you’ve spent 73% of the plan.”

A visually light style

The interface is clean: the screen shows the priority numbers and accents.

Growth potential

AI suggestions

Suggest a budget for next month based on past spending.

Monetization

• A free basic version. • A subscription for family mode, advanced analytics, reminders, and export.

Integrations

• Import from bank SMS or an API. • Calendar: it shows recurring payments.

Goals

Goals with visible progress, for example “save up for a vacation”.
